# 3.3 Professional development

## Summary

Development here means helping people do work they can get better at, with room to decide how, in service of something that matters. This page links to expectations and to the people guidance instead of repeating them, and keeps the growth practices the handbook already has. The commitments the organization makes, its stance on staff interviewing elsewhere, and any use of a keeper test are not yet decided, so they are marked as open.

## 3.3.1 Mastery, autonomy, and purpose {#mastery-autonomy-purpose}

The outline names three ideas as the frame for development: mastery (getting better at the work), autonomy (having a say in how it is done) and purpose (knowing why it matters). It does not say where the framing comes from or how the organization supports each.

::: clarify
Needed: what each of mastery, autonomy and purpose means in this organization's work, how the organization supports each, and the source of the framing if one is intended. No research claim is made here until a source is chosen. Purpose may connect to the shared beliefs in the DNA chapter, but that link is not assumed.
:::

## 3.3.2 Expectations and compensation as context {#expectations-and-compensation}

Development happens against what is expected of someone. Read [3.1 Expectations](expectations.md) for the Individual Contributor, Directly Responsible Individual and Player-Coach roles and the draft guidance on performance; the maintained rubrics live in the appendices and are not recreated here. Compensation is covered in [3.2 Compensation](compensation.md), where most policy inputs remain open.

Performance, promotion and pay are separate questions. How development conversations relate to them is not decided; see 3.1 for what the current draft says.

## 3.3.3 Development conversations and opportunities {#development-conversations}

The handbook already describes growth through responsibility: choosing a next step that stretches skills while fitting capacity. That text is at [2.3.4 Supporting growth without assuming rapid transformation](../leadership/people.md#supporting-growth). Feedback culture is at [2.1.1](../leadership/culture-in-leadership.md#feedback-culture).

The 10-2 performance notes suggest brief development discussions in regular 1:1s and feedback as events happen; this is a proposal, not an established schedule (see 3.1).

::: clarify
Needed: how development conversations work for staff in practice, who holds them, what opportunities a small organization can realistically offer, and what the organization commits to. This draft makes no development commitments.
:::

## 3.3.4 Interviewing elsewhere {#interviewing-elsewhere}

::: clarify
The outline describes an intended explicit invitation to explore other jobs, given limited organizational growth, but the wording is unfinished. Needed: your finished wording, how this works in practice (for example, whether and how staff tell the organization), and what the organization does and does not promise. No stance is stated in this draft.
:::

## 3.3.5 Netflix influence and the keeper test {#netflix-and-keeper-test}

The [Netflix Culture Memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture) is listed in the handbook's reading leads as an example of a high-autonomy culture. An external employer's practices are an influence, not automatically Sapiens First policy.

::: clarify
Needed: your intended philosophy, whether the keeper test affects retention at all, who would apply it, how, and what commitments accompany it. This draft does not describe the keeper test or adopt it. The Netflix reference is intended for the reading list at C.3 once the philosophy is decided.
:::

## 3.3.6 Transitions and handoffs {#transitions-and-handoffs}

This section is proposed. Leaving well means the work continues. The existing project guidance on handoff covers sharing finished work and its limitations, organizing files and decisions, recording lessons, naming who maintains what, and updating the work record: see [Finish and hand off](../leadership/strategic-planning.md#project-execution-and-handoffs). Handing a role over is described in [Roles and circles](../dna/structure.md#roles-circles-decision-rights).

::: clarify
Needed: whether a canonical handoff template exists or is wanted, and what applies specifically when a staff member leaves (for example notice, knowledge transfer or access). No staff-departure process is stated here.
:::
