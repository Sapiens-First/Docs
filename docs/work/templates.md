# Templates

Use these as starting points. Keep what helps, adapt the examples, and leave out sections that don't serve the work. Store completed records with the project and link them from Atlas.

## Project scope

Based on the Fellowship's scope template.

| Field | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Project | Name, owner, team, and timeframe |
| Objective | The change you want to achieve and why it matters |
| Audience | Who will use or benefit from the result |
| Success criteria | How you will know it worked |
| Outputs | The concrete things that will exist |
| Key questions | Tradeoffs, assumptions, and uncertainties to resolve |
| Scope | What is included and explicitly excluded |
| Constraints | Time, capacity, budget, dependencies, and other limits |
| Decision rights | What the owner decides, where consultation is needed, and who approves reserved decisions |
| Milestones | Meaningful results or decision points on the way to completion |

::: details Example: a first-meeting guide

**Objective:** Help a new organizer run a welcoming first meeting.

**Output:** A short guide and sample agenda.

**Success criteria:** Agree criteria with the project contact; for example, a new organizer can use the guide to prepare and run a pilot meeting, and identify a next step with participants.

**In scope:** Invitations, preparation, facilitation, and follow-up.

**Out of scope:** A complete chapter constitution or a full training curriculum.

**Key question:** What do first-time organizers find hardest?

**Milestones:** Understand the needs; draft the guide; pilot it; revise and hand off.

This is an illustrative scope, not a current assigned project.
:::

## Project plan

```text
Objective:
Final output:
Success criteria:
Owner and decision holders:
Available capacity and deadline:
Important assumptions or open questions:

By [date]: [specific milestone outcome]
- [ ] [Concrete next action]
- [ ] [Feedback, test, or decision needed]
- [ ] [Follow-up action]

Completion and handoff:
- [ ] Review the output against the success criteria.
- [ ] Organize files and essential instructions.
- [ ] Record remaining limitations and next steps.
- [ ] Confirm who receives or maintains the work.
```

::: details Optional: use an AI assistant to help plan

The original Fellowship handbook includes a detailed planning prompt. The reusable version below keeps its main constraints without tying them to one cohort's dates.

```text
Help me turn the project scope below into a realistic plan.

Use the stated objective, audience, outputs, success criteria, capacity,
deadline, dependencies, and decision rights. Do not invent missing facts,
approvals, stakeholder opinions, or research findings.

Identify important assumptions and add early actions to resolve them.
Organize work around meaningful milestones, each with specific actions.
Include an early useful version, feedback or testing, revision, and handoff
where relevant. Adapt the stages to the project.

Use only the update schedule provided. Milestones are checkpoints, not
extra reporting requirements. Do not add unnecessary documents or meetings.
Flag unrealistic scope and suggest what to reduce or defer.

Scope: [paste]
Available time and deadline: [fill in]
Agreed check-in / update schedule: [fill in]
```

Review the result yourself and with the relevant project contact. An AI-generated plan doesn't establish priorities or grant approval. Keep private organizational information out of tools unless their use is authorized.
:::
