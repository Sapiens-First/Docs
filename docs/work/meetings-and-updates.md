# Running meetings and writing updates

> Meetings and written updates keep work moving; choose the format that serves the purpose.

## In brief

- The existing Fellowship practice is weekly 1–1s with Rohan, plus either a team meeting or a written update every two weeks.
- A check-in covers progress, decisions, blockers, and support, and ends with what happens next and who owns it.
- A written update covers what was completed, links to evidence, risks or decisions needed, and what's next.
- General meetings are for connection and shared understanding of the vision, not project decisions.
- Your current program instructions hold the actual schedule that applies to you.

## When to use this

Read this page when:

- You're deciding whether a check-in, a written update, or a general meeting fits your situation.
- You're preparing a written update and want to know what to include.
- You're facilitating a meeting and want a simple structure to run it well.

Meetings and written updates help people make decisions, support one another, and keep work moving. Choose the format that serves the purpose: a check-in, a written update, or a general meeting.

## Project check-ins

Use a check-in to discuss progress, decisions, blockers, and support. Circles can also hold a short [metrics review](../learning/reviewing-metrics.md#a-regular-review). Share the relevant work beforehand when possible. Leave knowing what happens next and who is responsible.

The existing Fellowship practice includes weekly 1–1s with Rohan and either a team meeting or a written update every two weeks. Your current program instructions hold the actual schedule.

## Written updates

A useful update covers:

- What you completed and learned.
- Links to the work or evidence.
- Risks, blockers, or decisions needed.
- What you plan to do next.

You don't need to narrate every task. Help the reader understand the state of the work and where they can help.

## General meetings

General meetings help people connect, understand the movement, and discuss the vision and theory of change. They can include short teaching, small-group discussions, and a chance to share projects.

Keep agendas and notes with the current meeting records. The handbook holds the repeatable format; dated agendas are records of particular meetings.

::: roles Running a useful meeting

- State the purpose and prepare an agenda.
- Give someone responsibility for facilitation and someone responsibility for recording decisions.
- Make room for people who haven't spoken.
- Distinguish discussion from an actual decision.
- End with next steps, owners, and any dates that matter.

For a learning session, keep presentation time short enough for discussion and practice. For a project meeting, focus on the questions that need the group.
:::
