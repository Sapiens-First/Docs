# Feedback and development

People learn through practice, support, and honest feedback. We want people to take responsibility and ask for help when they need it.

Our [leadership standards](../organization/values.md#standards-for-leadership) describe the expectations we already set: reliability, openness to feedback, care, judgment, information sharing, and responsible handoffs.

## Give useful feedback

Describe what happened and its effect. Ask about the other person's perspective. Agree on a concrete next step rather than leaving them with a vague judgment.

If a commitment wasn't met, understand what got in the way. The response may involve clearer scope, more support, a smaller commitment, or a change in responsibility.

Metrics can show where someone might need support, but they don't replace this conversation. See [Metrics and people](reviewing-metrics.md#metrics-and-people).

## Develop through responsibility

A next step might be running part of a meeting, completing a project, supporting another Fellow, or coordinating a team. Choose something that stretches your skills while fitting your capacity.

Leadership includes making it easier for other people to contribute. Sharing context and handing work over well are part of that.

::: proposal Role-specific development rubrics

The notes propose rubrics for Fellows, managers, engineers, and other roles. A useful rubric would define observable expectations for the actual responsibility, with examples of good work and the support available.

Possible dimensions include quality, reliability, judgment, collaboration, and helping others develop. These are starting points for discussion, not a scoring system.

Before adoption, define who reviews whom, what evidence is used, how feedback is discussed, and what decisions the review affects. Separate a project's results from judgments about an individual's contribution: outcomes can depend on resources and circumstances outside their control.
:::
