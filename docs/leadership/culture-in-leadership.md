# 2.1 Culture in leadership

## Summary

Shared values become real in how leaders give feedback, handle disagreement, own their work, and set an example. This page covers the practices that exist today and marks the ones that still need an approved process. [1.3 Culture](../dna/culture.md) holds the beliefs; the standards themselves live in [A.1.1](../appendices/reference/values-standards.md).

## 2.1.1 Feedback culture {#feedback-culture}

Describe what happened and its effect. Ask about the other person's perspective. Agree on a concrete next step rather than leaving them with a vague judgment.

If a commitment wasn't met, understand what got in the way. The response may involve clearer scope, more support, a smaller commitment, or a change in responsibility.

Metrics can show where someone might need support, but they don't replace this conversation. See [Metrics and people](../learning/reviewing-metrics.md#metrics-and-people).

For practical guidance, including how people develop through responsibility, use the existing [feedback and growth guide](../learning/feedback.md).

## 2.1.2 Conflict resolution {#conflict-resolution}

Disagreement is part of Deliberation: we "disagree openly and incorporate feedback" ([1.3.1](../dna/culture.md#core-values)). When a disagreement stays stuck, or a concern involves the person you would normally go to, you need a clear route.

For now, use the existing guidance on [unclear responsibility](../organization/decisions.md#when-responsibility-is-unclear) and the reporting route in [A.1.1](../appendices/reference/values-standards.md#red-lines) for conduct that crosses a red line.

::: clarify
No approved conflict resolution process exists in the source documents. Still needed: how mediation works and who mediates; the steps for escalation; an alternative reporting contact when a concern involves the usual contact; who handles reports; and how review or appeal works. This draft establishes none of these.
:::

## 2.1.3 Radical ownership {#radical-ownership}

::: clarify
This concept is planned but not yet defined. The intended meaning is still needed, along with its boundaries relative to actual role authority. Themes suggested for discussion: taking responsibility, making constraints visible, asking for help, and handing work over. They are not an adopted standard.
:::

Until then, the leadership standards in [2.1.4](#modeling-the-culture) already set expectations for taking responsibility and handing work over. Check [decision rights](../organization/decisions.md) before committing the organization; a change in this handbook's wording does not transfer authority.

## 2.1.4 Modeling the culture {#modeling-the-culture}

Leaders set the pattern others follow. These existing standards apply to people accepting formal responsibilities, and they have one canonical home in [A.1.1](../appendices/reference/values-standards.md).

Leadership also means making it easier for other people to contribute. Sharing context and handing work over well are part of that.
