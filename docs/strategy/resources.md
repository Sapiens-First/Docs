# Sapiens First reading list

> Reading on our position, on movements, and on measurement — useful, not required.

## In brief

- You don't need to work through this list before you can participate.
- It covers our position, technology and society, movements, organizational culture, and measurement.
- Current policy recommendations and campaigns live on the Sapiens First website, not in this list.
- Including a resource here doesn't mean adopting every claim or tactic in it.

## When to use this

Read this page when:

- You want background reading on our strategy or position.
- You're looking for movement-organizing books referenced elsewhere in the handbook.
- You want the sources behind our approach to measurement.
- You want videos or short reads on technology, movement strategy, or how organizations distribute authority.

Start with the question you're trying to answer.

## Understand our position

- [Sapiens First policy](https://sapiensfirst.org/policy): current policy recommendations.
- [Sapiens First campaigns](https://sapiensfirst.org/campaigns): campaigns people can get involved in.
- [Atlas](https://sapiensfirst.org/atlas): how current work relates to the mission.

## Think about technology and society

- [*Why Privacy Matters*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pcSlowAhvUk), by Glenn Greenwald (video): why privacy matters even with "nothing to hide," and how surveillance can change behavior and constrain freedom.
- [*Slaughterbots*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9rDo1QxI260), by the Future of Life Institute (short film): a fictional look at the potential consequences of cheap, autonomous lethal weapons.

## Think about movements

- [Movement Action Plan](https://www.historyisaweapon.com/defcon1/moyermap.html): a framework used in Fellowship discussions about movement development.
- [Spectrum of Allies](https://spectrumofallies.com/): a way to think about people's different relationships to a cause.
- *Blueprint for Revolution*, by Srđa Popović: examples and ideas for nonviolent organizing.
- *Common Sense for the 21st Century*, by Roger Hallam: an argument for mass civil disobedience.
- [*Rules for Revolutionaries*](https://www.rulesforrevolutionaries.org/), by Becky Bond and Zack Exley: the "Big Organizing" model, which scales by giving volunteers real responsibility rather than centralizing everything in professional staff.
- [*How to Start a Revolution in 2026*](https://rogerhallam.com/how-to-start-a-revolution-in-2026/), by Roger Hallam: a contemporary framework for building political power through organizing, mobilization, structure, and institutional change.
- [*How to Topple a Dictator*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uo7w3g24DWo), by Srđa Popović (video): a practical introduction to nonviolent movements: broad participation, strategic wins, humor, and understanding where power comes from.

## Think about measurement

- *High Output Management*, by Andy Grove: leading indicators, pairing measures, and objectives and key results.
- *Working Backwards*, by Colin Bryar and Bill Carr: how Amazon separates controllable inputs from outcomes and reviews metrics every week.

See [Metrics](../learning/metrics.md) for how we apply these ideas.

## Think about organizational culture

- [*Holacracy*](https://www.holacracy.org/): a model for distributed authority using explicit roles, clear ownership, and less traditional hierarchy.
- [Netflix Culture Memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture): a high-autonomy culture built around strong talent, context-sharing, candid feedback, and fewer unnecessary rules.
- [*From Hierarchy to Intelligence*](https://block.xyz/inside/from-hierarchy-to-intelligence), by Jack Dorsey and Roelof Botha: a model centered on individual contributors, directly responsible individuals, and player-coaches instead of traditional management layers. It argues AI can take on much of the information routing that management layers exist for.

## Explore with an LLM

You don't need to read everything first. Give an LLM the title, link, or text and ask:

- "What are the 5 main ideas?"
- "What assumptions or weaknesses does this have?"
- "How would we apply this in an organization?"
- "Compare this with Holacracy, Big Organizing, or Netflix."
- "Quiz me until I understand it."

Treat the LLM as a sparring partner, not just a summarizer.

These resources offer perspectives to discuss and evaluate. Including a resource doesn't mean adopting every claim or tactic in it.
