# Learning resources

Start with the question you're trying to answer. You don't need to work through a reading list before you can participate.

## Understand our position

- [Sapiens First policy](https://sapiensfirst.org/policy): current policy recommendations.
- [Sapiens First campaigns](https://sapiensfirst.org/campaigns): campaigns people can get involved in.
- [Atlas](https://sapiensfirst.org/atlas): how current work relates to the mission.

## Think about movements

- [Movement Action Plan](https://www.historyisaweapon.com/defcon1/moyermap.html): a framework used in Fellowship discussions about movement development.
- [Spectrum of Allies](https://spectrumofallies.com/): a way to think about people's different relationships to a cause.
- *Blueprint for Revolution*, by Srđa Popović: examples and ideas for nonviolent organizing.
- *Common Sense for the 21st Century*, by Roger Hallam: an argument for mass civil disobedience.

These resources offer perspectives to discuss and evaluate. Including a resource doesn't mean adopting every claim or tactic in it.

::: details Further reading from the original movement guide

The original guide also recommended *The AI Revolution* by Tim Urban, *AI 2027*, *The Adolescence of Technology* by Dario Amodei, and resources about citizens' assemblies and international AI governance.

Specific research claims and reading links should be checked before they're used in training or public materials. When sharing a source, explain the question it helps answer and any assumptions that matter.
:::
