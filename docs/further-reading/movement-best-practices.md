## B.4.1 Nonviolent organizing {#nonviolent-organizing}

- *Blueprint for Revolution*, by Srđa Popović: examples and ideas for nonviolent organizing.
- *Common Sense for the 21st Century*, by Roger Hallam: an argument for mass civil disobedience.
- [*How to Start a Revolution in 2026*](https://rogerhallam.com/how-to-start-a-revolution-in-2026/), by Roger Hallam: a contemporary framework for building political power through organizing, mobilization, structure, and institutional change.
- [*How to Topple a Dictator*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uo7w3g24DWo), by Srđa Popović (video): a practical introduction to nonviolent movements: broad participation, strategic wins, humor, and understanding where power comes from.

## B.4.2 Recruitment, participation, and movement development {#recruitment-participation-and-movement-development}

- [Movement Action Plan](https://www.historyisaweapon.com/defcon1/moyermap.html): a framework used in Fellowship discussions about movement development.
- [Spectrum of Allies](https://spectrumofallies.com/): a way to think about people's different relationships to a cause.
- [*Rules for Revolutionaries*](https://www.rulesforrevolutionaries.org/), by Becky Bond and Zack Exley: the "Big Organizing" model, which scales by giving volunteers real responsibility rather than centralizing everything in professional staff.

For training resources, see [Training organizers](../appendices/reference/field-guides/training.md).
