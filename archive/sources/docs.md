Sapiens First

We imagine a world where AI serves the common good. We believe in technology that uplifts all people, not just a rich few.

We fight for democracy, prosperity, and security in the age of AI.
Background

Powerful artificial intelligence is coming this decade. Such power could:

    Upend our economic system.
    Empower dictators and oligarchs.
    Annihilate the human race.

We call this the “AI Crisis.”
Objectives

Our current mission is to mobilize 3.5% of the population and create dramatic political change this decade.

On policy, we have three core focus areas: democratic renewal, common prosperity, a secure future. Our specific recommendations can be found at sapiensfirst.org/policy.
Approach

Political systems change 3.5% of the population actively participates in a movement.

The ART model describes a virtuous cycle of civic engagement:

    Act. An action is anything that inspires others to participate – whether that’s hosting a picnic, door-knocking session, or peaceful demonstration.
    Recruit. One-on-one conversations are the core of our efforts to invite others to join the fight against the AI Crisis.
    Train. Our workshops & trainings help people learn the best ways to act.

Our strategy is drawn from a lineage of powerful, viral social movements such as the Tea Party Movement, Extinction Rebellion, and Otpor.

Core Values

Anyone can act in the name of Sapiens First if they follow our values:

Peace. We are peaceful activists! We do not harm people or property, or threaten to do so.

Wisdom. We believe the fight for democracy is a marathon, not a sprint. This means taking care of ourselves and others.

Deliberation. To create change effectively, we deeply consider a range of possible tactics. We disagree openly and incorporate feedback from others.

Respect. We are kind, listen, and use non-violent communication. Sapiens First members should also not violate the “red lines”, which are defined below.

Agency. When we feel strongly, we take action!
Operating Principles

To encourage creative, sustainable, and organized activism, we have the following operating principles.

Abundant Leadership. Everyone can be a leader in the movement! Primarily, this means proactively defusing conflict, and thinking about strategy.

Distributed Responsibilities. Roles and responsibilities should be equitably distributed, to reduce bottlenecks

Tactical Freedom. We agree on our Values and campaigns. Within those boundaries, teams may creatively use various tactics to advance the mission.

Collective Learning. Teams are encouraged to test new ideas. They should share what worked, what failed, and what others can learn, including through written documentation.
Standards for Leadership

Movement Leads (including Fellows and staff) accept formal responsibilities on behalf of the movement. We expect Leads to:

    take their responsibilities seriously;
    do what they say they will do;
    welcome feedback and listen to disagreement;
    care for the people they work with;
    use good judgment;
    share information and avoid becoming a bottleneck;
    put the success of the movement ahead of personal status;
    step down or hand over a role when they can no longer fulfill it.

Political Stances

Currently, Sapiens First does not write official endorsements for political candidates or coalition statements.

Members of Sapiens First are, in an individual capacity, welcome to

    hold a wide range of political views;
    participate in other movements and organizations;
    attend outside protests and events;
    sign petitions and open letters in their personal capacity;
    work with other groups on shared goal

In the future, we imagine that Sapiens First chapters may issue endorsements / official statements following a democratic process from chapter members.
Red Lines

People acting in the name of Sapiens First must not:

    harm or threaten to harm people or property;
    bully, harass, intimidate, or discriminate against others;
    deliberately humiliate people;
    knowingly create serious risks for participants or the public;
    misrepresent Sapiens First’s Demands, Values, activities, or relationships;

People who violate these Red Lines may be asked to leave an event, step down from a role, or stop acting in the name of Sapiens First.

Please report members crossing redlines to rohan@sapiensfirst.org. Following a report, leadership will interview both parties and other members to make a decision about next steps.
Starting a Circle

Starting a Circle takes just 3-5 hours a week! It can be a huge boon to others in your area looking to take action on tech & democracy.
Getting Started

To start, do the following:

    Join Sapiens First. Becoming a Member means that you get support from the Sapiens First Global team, and can get advice from other folks who are starting groups in their area.
    Get two friends to join you (recommended). Having friends can be extremely helpful if they are reliable and care about the issue as much as you. If you don’t have those people, don’t worry – you will find them along the way! 

Core Functions

We suggest focusing on three domains when you’re starting out:

    A general meeting.
        Choose a place and time to hold general meetings to invite other people to.
        The meetings should be uplifting, meaningful, and encourage broadbase participation. When someone comes to a meeting, invite them to be involved in planning an action (eg. recruitment event) – with the hope that they take on a Role in the future.
        General meetings can be held in a public library, a park, someone’s home, a school classroom.

    A recruitment tactic.
        Starting out, you may use a single tactic to recruit.
        Door-knocking is a great tactic, since it’s infinitely repeatable and highly effective (though time-intensive). Eventually, though, you could have your recruits do door-knocking and grow exponentially!
        You could also put ads online, or in 

    A priority campaign.
        You can choose a campaign that you believe will resonate in your community. Having a campaign gives you something concrete to say when people ask, “what do you do?” In the beginning, you can have your goal to get more people to sign the open letter for your chosen campaign – then, use that as a way to invite people to your general meetings.
        As you get more people, you can also run multiple campaigns at once, or do other tactics like protests that get more attention.

Holacracy

People thrive when they have autonomy, mastery, and purpose. Holacracy is a way of running an organization that gives every purpose those things – leading to effective advocacy without straining a small group of people.

If you think about the human body – every cell has shared “DNA” that gives them instructions on how to act. But there is no one leader – rather, every cell knows what functions they have to perform.

In Holacracy, an organization follows a Constitution, rather than a CEO. This means there’s distributed authority, which makes for faster and more effective decisions made by the people with the right context.

Each person may have multiple roles, and each role has certain expectations. By making these roles explicit, everyone knows what’s expected of them by consulting a central source of information. Should tensions come up – or a domain comes up for which there is no owner – this is addressed periodically at a governance meeting.

What this all means is that a Circles can grow into larger organizations without one person being a key, irreplaceable leader. Since authority is distributed, your local group can grow rapidly and recruit people to fill new roles when your group wants to take on bigger challenges.

Roles in Circles

A Circle is a fundamental organizational unit. Each Circle has, at a bare minimum, the following roles:

    Facilitator – Ensures meeting go according to agenda.
    Secretary – Calls meetings.
    Outer Link – Communicates with Sapiens First Global
    Governance – Assigns roles.
    Function 1, 2, 3 etc. – People responsible for the Circle’s function

One person will likely have multiple roles. For example, imagine you have Alice, Bob, and Carol who want to start a Circle in Berkeley:

    Facilitator – Alice
    Secretary – Bob
    Outer Link – Carol
    Governance – Alice
    Recruitment (Function 1) – Bob
    General Meeting Agenda (Function 2) – Carol

Outreach
Why door-knocking?

 Door-knocking is vital for the growth of our movement. Three reasons:

    Empowerment. It empowers others to engage on the issue.
    Network effects. It’s an invitation to participate. If they get really involved and bring their friends, this allows our political strength to grow exponentially.
    Depolarization. With a one-on-one conversation, we can find common ground in a way that much harder through the news or social media.

A successful door-knocking conversation goes as follows:

    Knock on the door of a stranger in your neighborhood. By connecting with a stranger, you expand your network and tap into theirs.
    Listen most of the time (80%). When people are listened to, they feel heard and cared for. This is empowering.
    Invite the person to share more at an upcoming online meeting.

Disruptions

Anyone can design an action provided it follows the Sapiens First values. In this section, we give some suggestions to get you started.

To plan an action, activists simply decide:

    How
        With humor, drama, or sacrifice.
        Disruptive to at least some extent

    What
        Confront a politician OR
        Disrupt a public event

    When
        City councils, town halls, candidate forums OR
        University campuses, farmer’s markets, public transit

Green

Green actions are low-cost, easy, and only need two people. These are typically best done at a government meeting or town hall. Examples:

    Pajama Party. Hold pillows and speak-out about how your politicians are “sleeping at the wheel” amidst the AI Crisis
    Hold A4 paper and speak about how it represents the government’s inaction
    Bring towels and say that you refuse to “throw in the towel” when it comes to AI
    Hold a trash bag and throw in bits of paper that represent our rights in the event of AI takeover
    Write a letter that threatens legal action against your representative for criminal negligence on the AI crisis
    Chalk “Declare, Democratize, Govern” around your local area

Yellow

Yellow actions are more disruptive or take more people. These can be done at a public meeting or in a public gathering place. Examples:

    Barney
        Get a costume of Barney or another dinosaur
        Go on BART trains and speak out about the Crisis
        “Gavin ❤️ AI / We all die / sapiensfirst.org” 

    Die-in/funeral for individuals or our future. Place old shoes on stage.
    Banner drop over a freeway
    Dance party at a major intersection or townhall 

Red

Red Actions are the most disruptive but also take personal sacrifice or large numbers of people. These can be done at a government meeting, a public space, or in front of an influential institution. Examples:

    Hunger strike
    Blockade a major road for a day
    Sit-in at a data center

While doing an action, you may designate 1+ people to photograph/video, or invite a journalist to join.
